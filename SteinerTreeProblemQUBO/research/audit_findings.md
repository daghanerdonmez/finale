# Formulation, implementation, and benchmark audit

Date: 2026-09-23. This note audits the current checkout; it cannot establish which historical version produced the report's figures. Existing files were not modified during this audit.

## 1. The depth construction is mathematically viable

With nonnegative edge costs and sufficiently strong positive penalties, the report's zero-penalty assignments encode rooted Steiner trees. Every selected arc strictly increases an integer rank, hence selected directed cycles are impossible. Every non-root terminal has one parent; every used nonterminal has one parent; unused nonterminals cannot have children. Following parents must therefore terminate at the sole allowed root. Conversely, orienting any Steiner tree away from its root and assigning graph distances from the root yields a zero-penalty extension.

For B=ceil(log2(n)), R=2^B−1 and M=R+1, the implementation's rank range is adequate. Its inactive-arc slack lies in [0,2R], and B+1 binary slack bits suffice. The report's ceil(log2(2n+1)) slack-bit expression gives one more bit than necessary when n is a power of two, but this does not invalidate feasibility. Root bits can be fixed to zero rather than penalized. Strictly increasing ranks do not themselves require a root-depth penalty; fixing the root removes redundant freedom.

The report's later exact variable count nevertheless uses B rather than B+1 slack bits per arc; the current builder has A(B+2)+nB+(n-k) variables, where A=2m-d_r. Its per-square clique count similarly needs 3B+2 rather than 3B+1 variables. Summing those clique counts also repeats shared depth-bit interactions, so it is a count of generated contributions / an upper bound, not an exact count of unique nonzero assembled interactions. The stated asymptotic upper bounds survive these corrections.

## 2. The barrier problem is structural

For one arc, let r=o_v−o_u−1+M(1−e)−g. Starting at r=0, flipping e alone changes this squared penalty by exactly M². Thus the depth part contributes λ_depth M² to the single-bit edge-flip energy change, independently of the graph cost. The highest slack bit has weight M, so its flip creates the same penalty. Flipping rank bit b contributes λ_depth 4^b for each incident arc whose equality was satisfied. Inactive arcs participate too: they do not constrain ranks logically, but their slack certificates resist rank changes dynamically.

This is stronger evidence than large coefficient magnitudes alone. There is a mathematically correct zero set, but local moves are forced to repair multiple certificate bits while crossing large penalties. Replacing only the slack/rank expansion with unary variables does not eliminate the M-sized edge switch. Dividing only depth penalties by M² reduces their smallest nonzero violation gap by M²; restoring the old exactness guarantee restores the large coefficients. Dividing the whole Hamiltonian by a constant leaves relative gaps and barriers unchanged, though it can improve an otherwise mismatched fixed solver schedule.

A safe exact penalty is λ>U for every nonnegative integer-valued penalty component, where U is the cost of any known feasible Steiner tree. Then every infeasible assignment has energy >U and a feasible assignment of energy U exists. U can come from a cheap heuristic or a pruned spanning tree; the loose fallback (n−1)c_max is sufficient for connected graphs. The objective must use nonnegative costs for this argument.

One cannot universally replace this by λ>c_max: take a path of n vertices with only its endpoints terminal and every edge cost c. The optimum is (n−1)c. The empty edge set, all ranks zero, p=0, and g=M−1 violates just the non-root-terminal parent constraint and has energy λ. Thus any λ<(n−1)c makes this infeasible state better than the optimum.

## 3. Current implementation differs from the report

- `MyFormulation/steiner_to_bqm_hybrid.py:18–22` sets all five penalty weights to 1.0. Its builder at line 25 accepts only `problem`. For typical input costs 1–100, unit penalties do not in general encode the optimum correctly.
- `AlexFowler/steiner_to_bqm_alex.py:58` instead defaults to P=(n−1)c_max+1. Comparing these builders' defaults is not a like-for-like penalty comparison.
- `MyFormulation/steiner_to_oj_qubo_hybrid.py:3–6` imports a nonexistent `MyFormulization` package; the actual directory is `MyFormulation`. Line 20 passes a second positional penalty argument to a builder that accepts only one argument. The ordering wrapper also has a stale `MyFormulization` import. Several benchmark scripts import the ordering solver from that nonexistent package too.
- `AlexFowler/oj_solver_alex.py:36` passes `version` to its wrapper, but the current wrapper accepts only problem and constraint weight.
- `diagnostics/qubo_diagnostics.py:229–231` builds a hybrid nonterminal-parent term multiplied by n, while the current builder applies neither that factor nor the diagnostic global penalty. The diagnostic full-builder call is also incompatible with the current signature.

These are reproducibility problems in the current checkout. They do not prove the historical experiments used the wrong weights.

The newer `notebooks/hybrid_alex_gurobi_openjij_comparison.ipynb` does bypass the stale wrappers. It normalizes costs, explicitly overrides the hybrid parent/usage/root weights to 5 and depth weight to 1, and sets Fowler's weight to 5. Thus the module defaults are not the weights actually used in that notebook. Its per-term weighting needs its own correctness analysis; the module-default mismatch must not be used to dismiss the notebook's observed difficulty.

## 4. Benchmark metrics need correction before new conclusions

Both `benchmark/new_models_benchmark_segment2.py` and `benchmark/new_models_geo_benchmark_segment2.py` have the following issues in `_run_model`:

1. Lines 141–143 set `cost = energy + offset`. This is the entire penalized Hamiltonian, not the selected-edge objective. Approximation ratios computed from it cannot be interpreted as Steiner-tree approximation ratios. A structurally valid optimal tree with incorrect auxiliary bits is treated as suboptimal.
2. Lines 145–154 update the minimum before checking feasibility and classify success using only `best_cost <= opt_cost`. An infeasible low-energy state can count as solved if penalties are insufficient. A correct success test is graph-feasible and decoded edge cost equal to the optimum within numerical tolerance. Independently report whether the full QUBO certificate is feasible.
3. `response.data()` defaults to energy-sorted order, not generation order. Consequently the claimed first-hit read is a rank within a batch. This is verified by the [dimod API documentation](https://docs.dwavequantum.com/en/latest/ocean/api_ref_dimod/generated/dimod.SampleSet.data.html). Use `sorted_by=None` and preserve chronological records, or record hits online. Honor `num_occurrences` if samples are aggregated.
4. All 100 reads have already completed before line 157 records the wall clock. Thus time-to-first-hit is batch-completion time. It should be labeled as such or measured per read. Standard confidence-based TTS is a different statistic and should not be conflated with censored elapsed time.
5. `STOP_ON_FIRST_HIT=True` stops future batches for successful models. Feasible-read rates then use model-dependent budgets correlated with success, and the report's description of a fixed 1000-read experiment is not literally accurate. Fixed-budget experiments are easier to compare; early-stop experiments can be a separate metric.
6. `_is_feasible` deliberately collapses both directions of an edge and checks only the undirected tree. This is a legitimate decoded-solution metric, but it is distinct from parent/acyclicity/certificate feasibility and from a directed-edge objective that counts both directions. Report these separately.

The recorded experiments run `SQASampler`, which is simulated quantum annealing, not ordinary simulated annealing. In the pinned OpenJij 0.10.17 [source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jij-Inc/OpenJij/v0.10.17/openjij/sampler/sqa_sampler.py), defaults are beta=5 and gamma=1 (lines 55–56), while the quartic schedule (lines 304–321) does not rescale them based on the model. The benchmark specifies sweeps and Trotter count only. Therefore models with coefficients of thousands versus millions face different effective thermal/transverse-field scales. Equal sweeps alone is not equivalent tuning. Normalize consistently, sweep temperature/transverse-field parameters on a separate tuning set, and log the actual schedule. This confound does not make the intrinsic M² barrier disappear.

An additional issue was confirmed experimentally after the initial audit: the installed OpenJij reuses a provided seed inside every read of a batch. Its SA initialization calls `gen_spin(seed)` each time; SQA does the same for each Trotter slice, and the base sampler invokes the seeded algorithm again at each read. The newer comparison notebook explicitly passes seed=0. `research/diagnose_openjij_seed.py` found 1 distinct state from 8 fixed-seed reads, compared with 8 distinct states from separate seeds, for both SA and SQA. Therefore those seeded notebook reads can repeat a trajectory rather than provide independent exploration. Use separate per-read seeds or an unseeded batch and measure diversity. This specific finding does not imply the older unseeded benchmark calls had the same issue.

## 5. Primary literature and novelty boundaries

- [Fowler, Improved QUBO Formulations for D-Wave Quantum Computing (2017)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/322634540_Improved_QUBO_Formulations_for_D-Wave_Quantum_Computing): author-uploaded thesis; Steiner-tree formulation in Section 5.2. This is the direct ordering baseline.
- [Rankooh and Rintanen, Propositional Encodings of Acyclicity and Reachability by Using Vertex Elimination, AAAI 2022](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/20530), [open PDF](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/20530/20289): preprocess a vertex-elimination graph and enforce local transitivity on elimination triangles. Low elimination width reduces encoding size; the paper gives soundness/completeness and experiments. This is directly relevant prior art for sparse ordering over chordal completions. A Steiner-specific QUBO adaptation may be useful, but sparse elimination-based acyclicity is not a new general idea. The targeted search did not find an identical Steiner QUBO construction; that is not a proof of novelty.
- [Karimi and Ronagh, Practical Integer-to-Binary Mapping for Quantum Annealers](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.01945), journal version Quantum Information Processing 18, 94 (2019): bounded-coefficient integer encodings explicitly trade extra bits for smaller encoding weights. Relevant controlled baseline; applying such an encoding alone retains the big-M edge coefficient.
- [Chancellor, Domain wall encoding of discrete variables for quantum annealing and QAOA (2019)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.05068): domain-wall discrete encoding and quadratic pairwise interactions. [Chen, Stollenwerk, Chancellor (2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.12224) gives empirical comparisons with one-hot, and [Berwald, Chancellor, Dridi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.12004) examines dynamics and encoding limits. Their performance results support testing domain walls; they do not establish that a particular Steiner encoding will outperform ordering.
- [Zaman, Tanahashi and Tanaka, PyQUBO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01708): QUBO logic constraints and arithmetic-circuit construction are standard techniques. A bitwise carry/comparator acyclicity construction should cite circuit-to-QUBO work and claim the graph-specific design and analysis, not invention of QUBO adders.
- [Liu and Dinneen, Solving the Bounded-Depth Steiner Tree Problem using an Adiabatic Quantum Computer (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1109/CSDE48274.2019.9162395): relevant prior depth-based Steiner construction, with cubic variable count in its abstract. Review before making broad novelty claims about depth labels.
- [Chitty, Charles, Moraglio and Keedwell, Comparing Quantum Annealer and Metaheuristic Methods to Solve the Steiner Tree Problem](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-07998-5_7): first online November 2025, proceedings citation year 2026. Relevant newer benchmarking paper; only its publisher abstract/reference list was inspected here, not the subscription full text.

## 6. Sparse ordering cross-check

For a chordal completion H of G without the root, use one orientation bit per edge of H, and penalize cyclic orientations of every triangle by Fowler's quadratic triangle polynomial. An orientation of a chordal graph is acyclic exactly when it has no directed triangle: a shortest directed cycle of length at least four has a chord, and whichever way the chord points it closes a shorter directed cycle, a contradiction. Any acyclic selected tree extends to a total vertex ordering, hence to an admissible orientation of H. Edge-orientation consistency and the report's nonnegative parent/usage terms then recover the rooted Steiner-tree constraints.

This proof is sound and avoids the large arithmetic coefficients. Width w gives O(nw) orientation bits and O(nw²) triangle penalties; parent terms still contribute O(sum_v degree(v)²), unless separately compressed. Sparsity by itself does not imply low width: sparse expanders may have linear treewidth and large fill. Benchmark by measured fill/triangle count and width as well as n and m, including paths, bounded-width graphs, grids, geometric graphs, random sparse graphs, and dense graphs. Do not delete all nonedge order variables without chordal completion: a chordless directed four-cycle has no triangle to penalize.
